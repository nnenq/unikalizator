DESCRIPTION
Inside San Quentin, California's oldest prison, a running club holds a full marathon every year without ever leaving the prison yard: 26.2 miles, 105 laps. One of its runners, Markelle "The Gazelle" Taylor, set the course record in January 2019, was paroled weeks later and ran the Boston Marathon a month after walking out. Three years later he ran it again, in 2:52.

In November 2025, 77 men started the 16th San Quentin marathon, and two of them broke three hours for the first time in the race's history, one second apart.

This video covers the history of San Quentin and its transformation into the San Quentin Rehabilitation Center, the 1000 Mile Club and its volunteer coaches, what a 105-lap marathon inside a prison looks like, Markelle Taylor's record, parole and Boston races, the pandemic years, the documentary "26.2 to Life", and the record-breaking 2025 race. Facts reported by a single source are attributed to it.
