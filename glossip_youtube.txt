DESCRIPTION
Richard Glossip was served what was supposed to be his last meal at least three times. Oklahoma set nine dates to execute him for the 1997 killing of motel owner Barry Van Treese. One was stopped hours before because of new claims about the case; another about an hour before, because the state had the wrong lethal injection drug.

The man who actually killed Van Treese, Justin Sneed, confessed and testified that Glossip told him to do it. Sneed got life. Glossip got death, twice. In 2025 the U.S. Supreme Court threw out his conviction, finding that prosecutors failed to correct Sneed's false testimony. On November 12, 2026, Glossip pleaded guilty to a lesser charge, accessory after the fact to first-degree murder. Sentencing is set for December 17-18, 2026.

This video covers the crime, the two trials, Oklahoma's lethal injection crisis and Glossip v. Gross, the last meals and last-minute stays, the wrong-drug scandal, the lawmakers' independent review, the attorney general who asked to vacate his own office's conviction, the 2025 Supreme Court decision, his release on bond and the November 2026 plea. Claims are attributed to their sources, and disputed points are marked. Barry Van Treese's death is the reason this case exists.
