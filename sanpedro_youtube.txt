DESCRIPTION
In most prisons, you are given a cell. In San Pedro prison, in La Paz, Bolivia, the first question is how much money you have.

Inside its walls, prisoners buy and rent their cells from each other, with estate agents, title deeds and section seals. There are rich neighborhoods and poor ones, elected committees, restaurants and football leagues. For years, children lived inside, and tourists paid to come in. In February 2026, Bolivia's prison director had to say it plainly: investments in cells create no property rights. This is the story of San Pedro. Disputed and unverified claims are marked as such.
