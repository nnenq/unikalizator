DESCRIPTION
In El Salvador's CECOT, the lights never go off, the bunks have no mattresses, and, according to the government, prisoners leave their cell for about thirty minutes a day. It was built for 40,000 men.

Almost everything known about daily life inside comes from two sources: guided tours run by the government, and the accounts of Venezuelan men sent there by the United States in March 2025 and released in July 2025. This video follows a day inside CECOT, from arrival to night, and shows where those two versions agree and where they don't. Each part is labeled on screen by its source: government, journalists and records, or former detainees. Allegations of abuse are allegations; El Salvador's government denies mistreatment.
